MASTER BUILD PROMPT — PHOTON DERIV TRADER

1. PROJECT IDENTITY

Build a professional automated trading application called Photon Deriv Trader.

The application will analyse and trade Deriv Synthetic Indices using a multi-timeframe market-structure methodology based on the educational material from Photon Trading.

The goal is to translate the concepts taught in the supplied Photon Trading videos into a structured, testable and eventually automated trading system.

This is not supposed to be a generic indicator-based trading bot.

The trading methodology, analysis engine, signal generation, risk management and execution engine must be separate modules.

Build the application as a functional product, not just a dashboard prototype.

2. TECHNOLOGY STACK

Use:

- Frontend: React + TypeScript.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS or an equivalent professional component system.
- Backend: Node.js + TypeScript.
- Database: PostgreSQL.
- Charts: TradingView Lightweight Charts or an equivalent charting library.
- API: Official Deriv WebSocket API.
- Authentication: Secure user authentication.
- Deployment: Replit.
- Mobile: Responsive, installable Progressive Web App (PWA).

Keep the architecture modular so the application can later be packaged as an Android application.

Never expose Deriv API tokens, credentials or sensitive settings in frontend code.

3. TRADING MARKET

The initial trading environment is Deriv Synthetic Indices.

Design the system so that supported symbols can be configured rather than hardcoded.

Allow the user to:

- Select synthetic indices.
- View live prices.
- View historical candles.
- Select analysis timeframes.
- Monitor market structure.
- Receive trading signals.
- Execute demo trades.
- Review trading history.

Do not assume Forex market sessions, institutional volume, real-world news or exchange order-book data exist in the same way on synthetic indices.

Validate every market-data assumption against the actual Deriv API.

4. PHOTON TRADING METHODOLOGY

The trading engine must be based on the following concepts identified from the supplied educational material.

A. Multi-timeframe market structure

Implement the identified timeframe hierarchy:

- Monthly over Weekly.
- Weekly over Daily.
- Daily over H4.
- H4 over H1.
- H1 over M15.
- M15 over M5.
- M5 over M1.
- M1 over 30 seconds.
- 30 seconds over 15 seconds.

Use this as a configurable timeframe hierarchy.

The initial primary analysis framework should focus on:

D1 — Overall market context

- Determine the broader market direction.
- Identify important structural highs and lows.
- Detect possible changes in the broader trend.

H4 — Market narrative

- Determine whether price is continuing the broader trend or retracing.
- Identify structural movements and relevant areas of interest.
- Determine where a continuation or reversal setup may develop.

M15 — Intermediate confirmation

- Analyse shorter-term market structure.
- Identify changes in direction.
- Look for evidence that an H4 retracement or continuation is developing.

M1 and lower — Entry refinement

- Examine local structure.
- Refine potential entry locations.
- Confirm whether the intended entry conditions are present.

Do not allow lower-timeframe signals to override higher-timeframe context without an explicitly defined strategy rule.

B. Strong and weak highs and lows

Implement a market-structure module that can identify:

- Swing highs.
- Swing lows.
- Strong highs.
- Strong lows.
- Weak highs.
- Weak lows.
- Higher highs.
- Higher lows.
- Lower highs.
- Lower lows.

A strong low is associated with a bullish impulse that moves price upward.

A strong high is associated with a bearish impulse that moves price downward.

Develop configurable structural detection algorithms.

Do not invent exact pivot lookback periods, swing confirmation rules or displacement thresholds. Store these as configurable parameters until they are verified against the original lessons.

C. Market structure and trend changes

Implement detection and visualisation of:

- Bullish market structure.
- Bearish market structure.
- Structural breaks.
- Potential trend changes.
- Confirmed trend changes.
- Continuation structures.
- Pullbacks and retracements.

Separate a potential trend change from a confirmed trend change.

Do not generate a trade simply because one candle breaks a previous high or low.

Require configurable structural confirmation conditions.

D. Liquidity concepts

Include a dedicated liquidity-analysis module based on the Photon Trading mechanical liquidity lesson.

The concepts identified so far include:

- Liquidity pools.
- Buy-side liquidity.
- Sell-side liquidity.
- Liquidity inducement.
- High-resistance liquidity.
- Low-resistance liquidity.
- Liquidity targets.
- Liquidity-based entry and exit planning.

The engine should identify potential liquidity areas from price structure.

Display these areas on the chart.

Do not invent the precise definitions or entry conditions for these concepts. Build the module so that verified rules can be added and tested later.

E. Supply and demand

Implement an area-of-interest module that can record and display:

- Supply zones.
- Demand zones.
- Potential order blocks.
- Relevant structural areas.
- Price reactions around identified zones.

Distinguish between detected zones and zones that have been confirmed by the trading methodology.

F. Premium and discount

Implement range-based premium and discount analysis.

The module should:

1. Identify a relevant price range using verified structural points.
2. Calculate the midpoint of the range.
3. Divide the range into premium and discount areas.
4. Display the equilibrium level.
5. Show whether price is currently trading in premium, discount or equilibrium.

The application should support the concept of looking for selling opportunities in premium and buying opportunities in discount, but must not execute trades based on this concept alone.

The exact range-selection rules must remain configurable until verified.

G. Fractal order flow

Implement a module that compares higher-timeframe and lower-timeframe structure.

For example:

- A higher-timeframe bearish trend may contain a temporary bullish lower-timeframe movement.
- That lower-timeframe movement may contribute to the formation of a higher-timeframe supply area.
- The application should distinguish a lower-timeframe retracement from a genuine higher-timeframe reversal.

Display this relationship clearly.

5. STRATEGY ENGINE

Create a modular strategy engine that combines the verified concepts.

Its workflow should be:

1. Retrieve market data.
2. Analyse higher-timeframe structure.
3. Determine directional context.
4. Identify relevant structural areas.
5. Analyse liquidity.
6. Analyse premium and discount.
7. Examine lower-timeframe confirmation.
8. Evaluate the entry conditions.
9. Pass the proposed trade to the risk engine.
10. Execute only if all required conditions pass.

Each strategy must have its own configuration and version.

Every generated signal must record:

- Symbol.
- Direction.
- Timeframe.
- Market structure.
- Higher-timeframe bias.
- Liquidity context.
- Premium/discount position.
- Relevant supply/demand area.
- Entry reason.
- Stop-loss logic, if defined.
- Take-profit logic, if defined.
- Invalidating conditions.
- Strategy version.
- Timestamp.

Do not manufacture entry, stop-loss or take-profit rules that are not established by the source material.

Create a strategy-rule configuration system so verified video lessons can be translated into exact machine-readable conditions later.

6. STRATEGY RULE VERIFICATION

Create a dedicated Strategy Documentation section.

For every implemented rule, store:

- Rule name.
- Description.
- Source video.
- Source timestamp or transcript reference, when available.
- Exact trading condition.
- Required confirmation.
- Invalidation condition.
- Implementation status.

Use three statuses:

1. VERIFIED — directly supported by source material.
2. PROVISIONAL — supported by identified teaching material but not yet confirmed against the full lesson.
3. UNVERIFIED — requires further source review.

Never present provisional or unverified rules as confirmed Photon Trading rules.

Allow the user to update a rule when more video transcripts become available.

Implement:

- API connection.
- Demo account authentication.
- Balance retrieval.
- Symbol information.
- Live tick subscriptions.
- Historical candle retrieval.
- Proposal requests.
- Buy and sell operations for supported contracts.
- Open contract monitoring.
- Contract settlement.
- Transaction history.

Handle:

- Connection loss.
- Reconnection.
- Authentication failures.
- API errors.
- Duplicate requests.
- Stale market data.
- Subscription cleanup.

Use the official Deriv API documentation to confirm supported request formats and contract capabilities.

Store API tokens securely on the backend.

Initially support demo accounts only.

8. RISK MANAGEMENT ENGINE

Create an independent risk management engine.

Include configurable limits for:

- Maximum stake.
- Maximum daily loss.
- Maximum consecutive losses.
- Maximum open positions.
- Maximum trades per day.
- Minimum time between trades.
- Maximum account exposure.

Include:

- Emergency stop.
- Automatic halt after risk-limit breaches.
- Duplicate-signal prevention.
- Stale-data protection.
- Connection-loss protection.
- Trade cooldown.

The strategy engine must never bypass risk controls.

Do not assume a fixed stake size is appropriate for every account.

9. BACKTESTING ENGINE

Build a historical backtesting module.

Allow users to:

- Select an index.
- Select a historical period.
- Select strategy version.
- Select timeframes.
- Configure risk parameters.
- Run historical simulations.
- View trade-by-trade results.

Calculate:

- Total trades.
- Winning trades.
- Losing trades.
- Win rate.
- Net profit or loss.
- Profit factor.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Average win.
- Average loss.
- Expected value.
- Consecutive wins and losses.

Use realistic historical execution assumptions.

Account for contract payout, duration, pricing and settlement characteristics where applicable.

Never assume that a candle touching a theoretical entry price guarantees a real Deriv contract would have been purchased at that price.

Clearly distinguish backtest results from live trading results.

10. PAPER TRADING

Before enabling real demo execution, build a paper-trading environment.

It should:

- Generate signals.
- Simulate trades.
- Record entries and exits.
- Calculate simulated profit and loss.
- Maintain a virtual balance.
- Produce a trade journal.

Allow strategy performance to be evaluated before connecting execution.

12. AUTOMATION MODES

Provide three operating modes:

1. ANALYSIS ONLY
   
   - Analyse markets.
   - Display structure.
   - Generate signals.
   - Never execute trades.

2. PAPER TRADING
   
   - Simulate execution.
   - Record performance.
   - No real contracts.

3. DEMO AUTOMATION
   
   - Execute approved signals on a Deriv demo account.
   - Apply every risk-management rule.
   - Provide a visible emergency stop.

Real-money trading must remain disabled.

Do not create a hidden mechanism that can bypass the mode restrictions.

16. IMPORTANT BUILDING RULES

- Do not build everything as one large React component.
- Use reusable components and separate services.
- Keep strategy logic independent of the interface.
- Keep market-data handling independent of trade execution.
- Write tests for market structure, liquidity detection, premium/discount calculations, risk controls and backtesting.
- Do not use random trading signals to make the dashboard look active.
- Do not claim profitability.
- Do not invent missing Photon Trading rules.
- Clearly label provisional features.
- Do not activate real-money trading.
- Do not use mock data as though it were live Deriv data.
- If an API feature is unavailable, explain the limitation and provide a safe alternative.

17. FIRST DEVELOPMENT TASK

Start by building PHASE 1 and the foundation for PHASE 2.

Before coding, inspect the project and provide a short implementation plan.

Then create the application structure, database schema, responsive navigation, dashboard and settings.

Ensure the app runs successfully.

Do not stop at a static design. Make navigation, controls and database-backed settings functional.

Once Phase 1 is complete, proceed to the Deriv market-data integration.

The final product should be a reliable, modular trading application that can progressively convert the actual Photon Trading methodology into testable automated trading rules.
